UGA Solar Infrastructure – Living Lab Project

Making harnessed solar energy more accessible and transparent

Overview

The 2026 Solar-Powered Table Capstone Team was tasked with upgrading the unused study table behind the Driftmier Engineering building, and we converted the scope to developing a living lab. By providing a blueprint for what features UGA engineering students want to see through surveys and telemetry, our team will provide both an upgraded table AND a compelling argument for what can be done all around campus.

These solar installations not only reduce the university's carbon footprint but also serve as educational tools, demonstrating the practical applications of renewable energy technology to students, faculty, and visitors. The Sunbolt tables around campus already harness the sustainable energy, but our testbed will provide more comfort and aesthetics to increase usage and retention, transparency to the inner-workings of the table, and long-term data on the effectiveness of our upgrades.

Impact & Next Steps

Our capstone team successfully transformed a standard solar table into a localized, transparent living lab. By integrating non-invasive telemetry and establishing a remote data pipeline, we laid the groundwork to track actual user engagement and energy consumption patterns. This data will inform future infrastructure decisions across campus, proving that aesthetic and comfort upgrades directly increase renewable resource utilization.

Consistent Occupancy Telemetry

We repurposed a Zigbee door contact sensor with a tiny magnet embedded inside 3-inch weatherproof cushioning to collect data on usage/seating. We tested that the magnetic field is strong enough to activate the glass reed magnetic switch inside the sensor when the cushion is compressed. In the future, we plan to improve reliability using an NPN transistor and a Force Sensitive Resistor (FSR) to track seating duration without unexpected outages.

Weatherproof Design

Our team increased comfort and aesthetics by building a new and improved backrest, installing detachable and weatherproofed 3-inch cushions. We've further improved the longevity of all electronic components by making encasings for the raspberry pi dashboard and 7-inch LCD touchscreen.

Remote Server & VPN Architecture

To ensure longevity and allow for off-site development, we deployed a Proxmox virtual environment on a repurposed laptop. By utilizing a Tailscale mesh VPN, our developer team can securely access the Home Assistant server remotely. This Flask web application is hosted on a VM and exposed via a Cloudflare tunnel, providing public access to the dashboard without compromising the school's network security.

Our team hopes that the College of Engineering @ University of Georgia will fully adopt our model for the Driftmier location so the remote server running the website and mesh VPN can be put onto the campus network. This will allow telemetry to run continuously on-site, and give our teams' efforts a chance to prove the effectiveness of our upgrades.
